**Verdict rules** (tail times with a count of 10 or more):
- *good:* ≥ 90% of the in-sample ratios are in the 95% band, and the out-of-sample typical error is at most 3 percentage points above the error of a correct model for the same pool;
- *fair:* ≥ 70% and at most 8 points above;
- *poor:* all other pools.

**What a correct model gives** (simulated Gompertz tails, 10,000 exceedances): 96–100% of the ratios in the band, a typical error of 1–2%, and a worst ratio at count ≥ 100 within approximately ±20%. 'Typical error' is the median |expected ÷ actual − 1|.

## 1. Shape above u: the QQ plot

Sort the excesses: how far each mark is faster than u. If the model is correct, the i-th excess is near the model quantile at (i − 0.5)/N_u. The figure plots the gap (data − model) against the model quantile, so a perfect fit is the zero line. The grey band shows how large the gap is by chance. To make it, 200 samples are drawn from the fitted model, rounded to 0.01 s, cut at u and fitted again, exactly like the data. A positive gap means the real marks are further ahead of u than the model says.

## 2. Counts: expected ÷ actual at every time in the tail

For each time t in the tail, compare two numbers:
- n·S(t): the number of athletes that the model expects at t or faster;
- c: the number of athletes that actually were.

If the model is correct, n·S(t) follows Gamma(c, 1). So the ratio has a known 95% band: approximately ×0.03–×3.7 at the record, ±20% at c = 100 and ±6% at c = 1,000. The curve must stay in the band and near 1. Times have only hundredths, so many athletes have the same time. The counts include these ties, and the model is read at t + 0.005 s.

Figures: `fit_quality_ratio_m.png`, `fit_quality_ratio_f.png`.

## 3. The formal test

The Anderson–Darling p-value (the ForwardStop test at the chosen u, times de-tied) is in the summary table. With thousands of exceedances, this test finds misfits that are too small to change a score. A small p tells you that the model is not exactly correct. It does not tell you how wrong it is. Sections 1, 2 and 4 measure that.

## 4. Out of sample

Fit 2025 alone (at the same quantile level as the stacked fit), then compare its expected counts with the counts of 2026. Then do the reverse. The 'typical error' in the summary is the median |ratio − 1| over the times with c ≥ 10.

**The yardstick.** The column 'the same for a correct model' repeats this test on two simulated seasons. Their fast tails are drawn from the stacked fit, at the real season sizes. So the seasons have no real difference, and the error that remains is only fit noise and count noise. The amount by which the real error is larger shows how much the two seasons really differ.

**Records** (division test, rule `gompertz-fs5`, 180 out-of-sample records, predictive p): KS p = 0.079, tails 3 / 9 (95% range 1-9), median z = 0.82 (0.69 if correct). Source: `comparisons/record_tests/record_calibration.md`, section D.
