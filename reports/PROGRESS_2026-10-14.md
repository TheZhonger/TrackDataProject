## 2. The models in one paragraph

All four describe the fast tail above a threshold u, on x = −seconds:
- **GPD**, the current model: has a wall when ξ < 0;
- **exponential**: the GPD with ξ = 0;
- **Weibull-type**: S ∝ exp(−((x−μ)/s)^θ), with tail weight θ (θ = 2 thins
  like a bell curve, larger is lighter);
- **Gompertz**, its θ → ∞ limit: S ∝ exp(−c(e^{y/b} − 1)).

The last three have no wall, and the Weibull and Gompertz have the "slide" in ξ
built in (`_config/evt_theory.md` §3.7). An earlier claim of mine was wrong: the
tail is **lighter** than normal, not normal. ξ fitted to the data sits about 0.12
below what a normal tail gives at the same exceedance fraction.

## 3. Benchmarks: Weibull / Gompertz vs GPD

### 3.1 Pooled northeast 2025 + 2026 and national boys 2026, 25 pools (`output/model_comparison/`)

| test | GPD | Weibull | Gompertz | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| best by AIC at the top 10% | 1 | 2 | **22** | Gompertz fits the shape best, with the GPD's parameter count |
| spread of a benchmark's 1-in-N across thresholds (median) | 1.60 | **1.22** | **1.22** | the answer depends far less on where you put u |
| out-of-sample log score, beats GPD (northeast, 20 season pairs) | n/a | 18 | 18 | consistent but small (≤ 0.011 nats per mark): in the body of the tail all three agree |
| marks in the other season past the model's wall | 4 | 0 | 0 | the GPD gave probability 0 to things that then happened |
| predicted ÷ actual at the other season's **record** (median) | 0.09 | **0.80** | 0.59 | the GPD calls next season's record about 11× rarer than it turned out |
| predicted ÷ actual at ranks 5 / 25 / 100 (median) | 0.87 / 1.20 / 1.15 | 1.01 / 0.98 / 0.99 | 0.98 / 0.98 / 0.99 | Weibull and Gompertz are calibrated to within 2% |

The exponential (ξ = 0) is far worse than all three (AIC +81 to +1,453), so the
tail is genuinely light, not "unbounded, exponential".

### 3.3 New today: do the intervals mean what they say?

This was computed for this report.
- **Method:**
  - fit each model on one northeast season (top 10%);
  - bootstrap it 100 times, re-deriving u each time;
  - for ranks k = 1, 5, 25, 100 of the *other* season, simulate the number of
    athletes at least that fast as Poisson(n × P̂);
  - ask whether the observed k lands inside the central 95% interval.
- **Scope:** 10 pools × 2 directions × 4 ranks = 80 checks per model.
- Script and output: scratchpad `coverage.py`, `coverage.csv`.

| model | covered (nominal 95%) | k = 1 | k = 5 | k = 25 | k = 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPD | **80%** (64/80) | 65% | 95% | 85% | 75% |
| Weibull | **98.8%** (79/80) | 95% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| Gompertz | **97.5%** (78/80) | 95% | 100% | 95% | 100% |

- **GPD misses (16):**
  - 7 are records the GPD put at or past its wall (predicted count ≈ 0, interval 0–0);
  - 5 are at rank 100, where it predicts too *many* athletes, so the observed
    100 falls below its interval (e.g. 1600m boys 2025→2026: interval 102–159,
    median about 127);
  - 4 are at ranks 5 and 25, in both directions.
  - It is wrong in both directions: too light at the record, too heavy at
    rank 100. That is the signature of the wrong shape.
- **Weibull's single miss:** 100m boys 2025→2026 at the record. It expected
  about 10 boys at least as fast as 2026's 10.44, but there was 1: 2025 was a
  faster season at the very top.
- **Caveat:** at k = 1 the Poisson part of the interval is wide, so that rank is
  a lenient test. The 80 checks also share pools, so they aren't independent.
  Slightly-above-nominal coverage (97–99%) means the intervals are, if
  anything, a little conservative.

## 4. Is the Weibull/Gompertz good enough to run inference on?

Graded by the kind of question you would ask it.

| inference | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| "How rare is this mark?" (1 in N), for marks at about rank 5 and slower | ✅ **yes** | calibrated to within 2% out of sample; stable across thresholds; 95% intervals cover at the nominal rate |
| Ranking marks **across events** by return period | ✅ **yes, at those ranks** | the point of the project. Return periods are unit-free and now come from a model that predicts the next season. Compare in units with the intervals attached |
| Confidence intervals on those return periods | ⚠️ **yes, with caveats** | coverage is 97.5–98.8% out of sample (§3.3). But: the row bootstrap only (stacked seasons need the athlete-cluster one); θ is weakly identified; Gompertz intervals aren't in the pipeline yet (only the scratch script) |
| How rare is the **record** / beyond-record extrapolation | ⚠️ **cautiously** | far better than the GPD (no wall, calibrated in 19 of 20 out-of-sample record checks), but the intervals are wide (e.g. US boys 100m 9.88: 1 in 458k, CI 167k–1.1M), and the girls' sprint records are mis-fit (predicted ÷ actual 4–5). Report records with intervals and a flag, not as headline numbers |
| A physiological limit / "wall" | ❌ **no** | these models have no wall by construction. And the GPD's wall is an artifact: it moved between seasons, and a 2026 mile beat 2025's whole wall interval. High school data cannot speak to the human limit |
| Formal goodness-of-fit p-values | ❌ **no** | Anderson–Darling is power-saturated at these sizes and rejects every model. Model choice rests on AIC, out-of-sample scores, calibration and coverage, which is what §3 does |
| Differences between states, seasons or regions | ⚠️ **descriptive only for now** | season-to-season return periods differ by up to 1.7×, and state medians are list-depth artifacts. Fitting per group needs the per-group sample sizes that only the large states have |

**Weibull or Gompertz?** Use the Gompertz:
- In 8 of 10 national pools, and most northeast ones, the Weibull ran to its
  Gompertz limit anyway.
- The two give the same return periods to within noise.
- The Gompertz has two parameters (c, b) with no flat ridge, so its parameters
  are interpretable and its bootstrap is better behaved.
- Its one weakness in the tests above is record calibration (0.59 vs the
  Weibull's 0.80), within noise for single records.

**Before switching scoring to it:**
1. Put the bootstrap (and the athlete-cluster version for stacked seasons) into
   the pipeline for the Gompertz. The scratch `coverage.py` already does the
   resampling loop.
2. Pick a threshold rule. Stability is good anywhere from the top 20% to the
   top 1%, so a fixed top 10% is defensible; state it, don't select it.
3. Re-run the coverage test with the **national 2025** data as the second
   season, once it is rescraped. That is the out-of-sample test the national
   fits currently lack.
4. Then switch `run_all.py`'s query table and cross-event scores to the
   Gompertz, keeping the GPD columns for comparison.
